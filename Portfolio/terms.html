FREELANCE ENGAGEMENT

Clear scope. Clear edges.

These plain-language notes explain how a project should be framed. They are not a service contract, legal advice, promise of availability or formal offer to supply work.

For a new project, begin with the work to be done, who will use it and any important constraints.

1. Estimates are not a signed scope

The package amounts, ranges, add-ons, support prices and delivery estimates on the pricing page were transcribed from the supplied previous portfolio configuration. That file has no verified last-review date. The figures are indicative and non-binding, are not a booking confirmation and may need correction before publication. A project-specific written proposal states the actual requirements, amount, currency, schedule, assumptions and exclusions.

Applicable taxes, transaction charges, third-party hosting, domain renewals, provider subscriptions, APIs and licensing are separate unless the written proposal expressly includes them. No discount, urgency, availability or launch date is implied.

2. What a project proposal should define

  • Users, goals, workflows, pages, roles, data and integrations in scope.
  • Deliverables, exclusions, assumptions, dependencies and acceptance criteria.
  • Milestones, review windows, payment schedule, price and currency.
  • How a requested change is described, re-estimated and approved before it affects delivery.
  • Who supplies and approves content, access, accounts, data and deployment credentials, and by what secure channel.
  • Testing boundaries, environment, deployment responsibility, handover and rollback approach.

The signed project agreement controls. A change is not silently added to the agreed work.

3. Payment and delivery timing

The previous portfolio file listed a suggested 50%/50% milestone pattern for website work and 40%/30%/30% for application/software projects, plus a possible 25% rush surcharge subject to confirmed availability. These are archived terms only: no payment percentage, rush slot or date applies unless it is reviewed and included in the accepted written proposal.

Any delivery estimate should state its unit, what event starts the clock, what content or approvals are needed and how waiting on a client or third-party provider affects dates. No timeline is guaranteed by this site.

4. Reviews, defects and ongoing support

The previous price data listed two review rounds and an indicated 14-day period for website tiers or 30-day period for application/software tiers. It did not have a verified last-reviewed date. The proposal must state what a review round means, how a defect is measured against the approved scope, when coverage begins and ends, and what is excluded. New features, content changes and third-party outages should not be treated as defects unless the written agreement says otherwise.

Optional monthly support is separate from a build. Any support agreement should define covered systems, work limits, availability, response windows, backup responsibilities, emergency handling, price, renewal and cancellation. A support plan is not automatically 24/7 monitoring or emergency response.

5. Accounts, data and third-party costs

The proposal should name the party that owns and pays for domains, hosting, email, APIs, software licences, merchant/provider accounts, backups and renewals. Prefer accounts controlled by the client or business. Never include account passwords or production credentials in this public site or a public repository.

Before transferring real customer or employee data, both parties should identify an approved transfer channel, permitted use, access controls, storage location and deletion/retention responsibilities. No client records, passwords or proprietary code from the supplied archives are bundled here.

6. Source code, copyright and licences

The project agreement should explicitly separate client-owned content and assets, newly created deliverables, developer tools or reusable components, third-party software, fonts and licensed assets. It should state when any licence or transfer takes effect and what open-source conditions remain. No blanket ownership transfer, exclusive licence or sublicensing promise is made by this information page.

7. Security and legal boundaries

Security activities are limited to the agreed build, dependencies and environment; the portfolio does not promise absolute security, bug-free software, legal compliance, tax treatment, merchant approval or continuous availability. Payment processing, consumer rules, privacy, employment records, invoicing and other regulated workflows require project-specific review.

For Indian engagements, ask a qualified legal and tax professional to assess applicable laws, including the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, DPDP Rules, 2025 and their applicable commencement provisions. For other jurisdictions, obtain relevant local advice. This page is not legal, tax or financial advice.

8. Questions before proposing work

To begin, email a concise description of the people involved, today's process, desired improvement, any system integrations and a preferred meeting window. Avoid sending credentials, financial account details or live personal records before an appropriate agreement and secure transfer plan exist.

Draft for owner review: Confirm all package prices, dates, milestone patterns, review/warranty language, current business identity and governing law with qualified advisers before a public commercial launch. A separate written contract should be used for paid work.
